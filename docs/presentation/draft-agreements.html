The draft agreements

Two for organizations, two for entrepreneurs — the four documents that make the federated network an agreement rather than a platform — reproduced in full, exactly as they appear in the running software.

These are drafts, and that is the point. Every word here is a starting position for the consortium to argue with. It is easier to respond to real language than to a blank page — so mark them up, strike what you disagree with, and bring it to the group. Nothing becomes v1.0 until the group says so.
Pilot versions, revised 24 September 2026. The two entrepreneur-facing documents were revised so their wording matches the privacy model in the deck — always shared with organizations the entrepreneur works with, only with consent for directory listing and record details, never shared for notes, financials and internal record IDs. Earlier drafts said directory information was visible to every organization in the network; it is now opt-in. These are the versions pilot partners sign and present to entrepreneurs. The membership terms were revised again on 25 September: the governance clause no longer promises each member a seat in a governance body, because how the network is governed has not been decided — it now states the intent (a nonprofit run for the benefit of its members, once formed) and that no member is bound by a version it has not signed and may leave at any time. The two entrepreneur-facing documents were revised the same day (compact 0.3, privacy notice 1.2): email is shared with an organization only once it needs it (when it accepts a referral), and never through the directory; directory listing is on by default and detail sharing off; the "why not a central database" argument was replaced by a plain statement that every member is approved and has signed not to sell or spam; and anonymous aggregate statistics are stated explicitly, since the network does publish them. Compact 0.4 adds how changes to the terms are handled: organizations sign every new version; entrepreneurs are told at their next visit and asked to agree again only when a change affects what is shared about them. A fifth document, the Referral Partner Terms (0.1), was added the same day for organizations that receive referrals without becoming members.

Joining the network: what your organization agrees to

Who signs: Each member organization, once per network it joins — signed by someone authorized to commit the organization.

The terms of joining. The other documents explain the network to entrepreneurs and govern how staff handle data day to day; this one states what an organization actually commits to in exchange for membership — and what it does not have to give up.

Who can join

Membership is open to organizations that provide direct support to entrepreneurs — support organizations, funders, and resource providers — operating in the region a network covers. An organization joins a network; it may join more than one, and its obligations are the same in each.

You keep your own systems and your own data

Joining does not require you to adopt anyone else's software, migrate your records, or hand over your database. You keep your own tools, your own record identifiers, and your own client relationships. You share only the agreed common fields, and only for the entrepreneurs you actually work with.

What you contribute

You agree to map your terms onto the shared data standard, to record referrals you send and receive through the network so they can be tracked to a conclusion, and to keep the status of your own records reasonably current. You are never obliged to collect data you do not already collect.

You honour the entrepreneur's choices

Consent travels with the person, not with your copy of their record. You agree to respect directory and sharing choices as they are recorded in the network, to seek consent before requesting another organization's operational data, and to stop using data an entrepreneur has withdrawn from sharing.

You answer referrals

A referral sent to your organization is a commitment made to an entrepreneur. You agree to accept or decline referrals within a reasonable period, to name someone responsible when you accept, and to record the outcome — including when the outcome is that you were not the right fit.

You protect your credentials

API keys and sign-in integrations are issued to your organization and identify every action it takes in the network. You agree to store them as secrets, to limit them to staff who need them, and to notify the network administrator promptly if one may have been exposed so it can be revoked.

How the network is governed

How the network is governed is not yet settled. The intent is for it to become a nonprofit organization run for the benefit of its members, which would then decide changes to the shared standard, to these agreements, and to who operates the network. Until it is formed, a proposed change is sent to every member and takes effect for a member only when that member signs the new version. You are never bound by a version you have not signed, and you may leave at any time.

Aggregate reporting

Anonymous, aggregate statistics — entrepreneurs served, businesses started and still operating, jobs, capital — may be published by the network to describe the ecosystem's impact. Individual entrepreneur data is never published, and no member may present the network's aggregate results as solely its own.

Leaving, and being asked to leave

You may leave a network at any time; your own systems and records are unaffected, your keys are revoked, and records you contributed remain subject to the entrepreneurs' own consent choices. Membership may be suspended for breach of this agreement or the data usage agreement — including misuse of another member's data or an entrepreneur's information — by decision of the network's governance body.

This is not an exclusivity arrangement

Membership does not restrict who you may serve, partner with, fund, or compete against. Nothing here obliges you to refer to any particular member, and the network takes no position on relationships between members outside it.

Document 2 of 5 · pilot 0.4 · entrepreneur-facing

Joining a shared entrepreneurship network

Who signs: Every entrepreneur, once, when their record first enters the shared network.

The plain-language explanation of what a federated network is and what joining it means. This is the document that has to be understandable, not just correct.

You are joining something shared by design

This system is part of a federated network of independent entrepreneurship-support organizations. Each organization runs its own tools and keeps control of its own data, and organizations that have signed the shared privacy and standards compact can exchange information about the entrepreneurs they support — with your consent.

What becomes visible across organizations

Always shared, with organizations you actually work with: your name, their own records with you, and the fact that other partners are helping you (who, what kind of support, when). Your email reaches an organization only once it needs it — when it accepts a referral for you, or because you signed up with it directly. Your choice: being listed in the network directory, where partners you have not worked with can find you (on unless you turn it off), and the details of one organization's records being seen by another (off unless you turn it on). Never shared: notes staff write about you and each organization's internal record numbers; the network does not collect financial information.

You stay in control of what moves

You are listed in the network directory unless you turn that off; sharing the details of one organization's records with another is off until you turn it on. Each choice applies to one network. A new organization asking to see another's records needs your approval. You can change any choice, or leave the network, at any time from your privacy settings, and doing so does not affect your relationship with any individual organization.

Who is in the network

Every organization in the network was approved by the network before joining and has signed the same membership and data-handling agreements: it may not sell your information, use it to send you unsolicited marketing, or use it for anything other than entrepreneurship support. No single organization holds everyone's data — each keeps its own records. An organization that breaks these rules is removed.

Anonymous statistics

The network publishes aggregate statistics — how many entrepreneurs were served, referred and helped, and where organizations record them, businesses started, jobs and capital. Your record counts toward those totals whatever your choices; the totals are anonymous and nothing published identifies you or your venture.

When these terms change

These terms will be improved as the network learns. The current version is always published on the network's terms page, and every organization in the network signs each new version before it applies to them. We ask you to agree again only when a change affects what is shared about you; otherwise we let you know the next time you visit, and your choices carry over unchanged.

What this organization will not do

We do not sell your data, we do not use it to send you unsolicited marketing, we do not share it outside the network without your consent, and we do not use it for purposes unrelated to entrepreneurship support.

Entrepreneur Data & Privacy Notice

Who signs: Every entrepreneur, alongside the compact.

The specifics: what is stored, who can see it, how cross-organization sharing is approved, and what rights the entrepreneur keeps.

What information is stored

When you participate in ecosystem programs, support organizations (ESOs) may record your basic profile (name, contact info, venture name and description), program participation and progress milestones, and interaction notes related to the services they provide to you.

Who can see your data

Organizations you work with see your name, the records they keep themselves, and the fact that other partners are also supporting you — which organization, what kind of support, and when — so they can coordinate rather than duplicate. Your email is shared with an organization only once it needs it: when it accepts a referral for you, or because you signed up with it directly. Organizations you have not worked with see only your directory listing — name and venture, no contact details — and only while you are listed.

Cross-organization sharing

The details of one organization's records — such as the name of a program you are in, or the outcome of a referral — are shared with another organization only if you approve it, either partner by partner or for every partner you work with in this network. Notes that staff write about their meetings with you are never shared with other organizations, even with your approval.

Your rights

You can see which organizations have access to your records, turn your directory listing off or on (it is on when you join), approve or revoke sharing, and leave the network altogether at any time from your privacy settings. Each choice applies to one network, and none of them ends your relationship with any individual organization. You can request a copy or deletion of your records by contacting your network administrator.

Anonymous statistics

The network publishes aggregate statistics — how many entrepreneurs were served, referred and helped, and where organizations record them, businesses started, jobs and capital — to show what the region's support organizations do together. Your record counts toward those totals whatever your choices; the totals are anonymous, small groups are suppressed, and nothing published identifies you or your venture.

What we do not do

Every organization in the network is approved by the network and has signed the same agreements: it may not sell your information, use it to send you unsolicited marketing, or use it for anything other than entrepreneurship support. Nothing is shared outside the network without your consent. Notes stay with the organization that wrote them, each organization's internal record numbers stay private to it, and the network does not collect financial information.

Document 4 of 5 · draft · staff-facing

Data Access & Responsibility Agreement

Who signs: Every member organization, and its staff who access shared data.

The day-to-day handling rules for people who touch other organizations’ data — the operational counterpart to the membership terms above.

Permitted uses

You may access entrepreneur information solely to provide ecosystem support services: reviewing venture details to offer relevant programs, recording interaction notes and program participation, and coordinating referrals with the entrepreneur's explicit consent.

Confidentiality obligations

You must treat all entrepreneur data as confidential. Do not share venture details, financials, interaction notes, or any personally identifiable information outside this platform or with parties not authorized to access it through this system.

Prohibited activities

You may not use entrepreneur data for purposes unrelated to providing ecosystem support. You may not share data externally, use it for commercial gain, or access records beyond what your role requires. You may not attempt to access data belonging to organizations outside your assigned scope.

Audit and accountability

All data access and modifications are logged and may be reviewed by ecosystem administrators. Access is scoped to your assigned organization and role. Administrators may audit activity at any time.

Reporting obligations

If you become aware of an accidental data disclosure or potential breach, report it immediately to your organization administrator. Any observed misuse of entrepreneur data should be reported through the platform feedback system.

Consequences of violation

Unauthorized use or disclosure of entrepreneur data may result in immediate access revocation, removal from this ecosystem, and potential civil or legal liability. This agreement survives the end of your participation in this ecosystem.

Receiving referrals from the network: referral partner terms

Who signs: An organization — typically for-profit: a law firm, an accountant, a marketing agency — that provides services entrepreneurs need and wants to receive referrals without becoming a member. Signed with the Data Usage Agreement, instead of the Membership terms and the Compact.

Nonprofits are service providers too; what sets a referral partner apart is not what it is but what it gets from the network: referrals, and nothing else. The terms let a for-profit firm be useful to entrepreneurs without the network becoming a marketing channel.

What a referral partner is

A referral partner provides a service entrepreneurs need — legal, accounting, marketing, design, manufacturing, or similar — and receives referrals from the network's members. It is not a member: it does not take part in the network's shared view of who is helping whom, does not see the directory, and has no role in how the network is run. Organizations that want that take part as members under the membership terms instead.

What you receive, and when

A referral tells you who is being referred, their venture, and why (the referring organization's introduction). Their contact details reach you only once you accept the referral. You see nothing else in the network: no other organization's records, no list of entrepreneurs, and no activity of other partners. Every referral is made with the entrepreneur's agreement.

What you may use it for

Only to provide the service the referral was for. You may not add a referred entrepreneur to a marketing list, send them unsolicited offers, sell or share their information, or pass them on to anyone else without their agreement. When your work with them ends, your use of what the network gave you ends with it; records you keep as their provider are governed by your own professional obligations.

No fees for referrals

Referrals are made on the entrepreneur's need, not on payment. No fee, commission, or other consideration passes to the network or to any member for a referral, and you may not offer one. You may charge the entrepreneur for your services on terms you agree with them directly.

What you commit to

You agree to accept or decline each referral within a reasonable period, to name who is responsible when you accept, and to record the outcome — including that you were not the right fit. You agree to keep any credentials the network issues you as secrets, and to tell the network administrator promptly about a possible exposure or misuse.

Anonymous statistics

Referral outcomes are counted in the anonymous, aggregate statistics the network publishes. Nothing published identifies an entrepreneur, and nothing about your individual clients is published.

Ending your participation

You may stop receiving referrals at any time, and the network may stop sending them at any time and for any reason; referrals already accepted are completed under these terms. Misuse of an entrepreneur's information ends your participation immediately and may be reported to the entrepreneur and to your professional body.
