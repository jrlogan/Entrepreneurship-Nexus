If you run an agency: what you do, what you get

The short version for a director deciding whether this is worth a staff meeting. Joining never means switching software — your system stays your system at every level.

What your organization would do

LevelYour effort
0 · Nothing changesKeep using email. Referrals to you arrive with accept/decline links — no login, no new tool, no integration.
1 · Refer through the networkStaff send referrals to a network address as they happen. Zero code. Records build one entrepreneur at a time from real activity — no bulk import of your database, ever.
2 · Connect your systemA one-time mapping of your fields to the small shared core (~1 hour), then your software sends the updates automatically when staff save a record (about an afternoon of developer time — a ready-made playbook, API spec, and AI scaffolding prompt are provided).
3 · Sign-in & live eventsOptional: offer "Sign in with [your org]" and receive real-time referral notifications into your system.

Plus, at any level: sign the compact and data-usage agreement — the same rules every member signs.

Benefit to your agency
  • Referrals that close. Every referral you send or receive has an ID, an owner, and a follow-up — no more wondering what happened to the person you sent across town.
  • Context without asking. When a shared client arrives, you see their journey (programs, memberships — never other orgs' notes) instead of starting cold.
  • The funding case, automatically. Aggregate numbers the state asks for — startups served, survival, jobs — fall out of the records you already keep.
  • Sovereignty intact. Your data, your IDs, your client relationships. Your key is revocable by you; leaving is clean; the software is open source, so the network can never be held hostage by one operator.
Benefit to the entrepreneur
  • Tell their story once. No re-entering basics at every door; a referral means the next org is expecting them.
  • Warm hand-offs, not cold tips. "Go talk to IP Factory" becomes an accepted referral with a named person and a date.
  • Real control. Directory visibility and deeper sharing are their choice, revocable, with deletion on request — spelled out in one plain-language agreement, the same at every member org.

Worried about competition? Nothing in the compact shares your pipeline, your notes, or your funder relationships. The shared layer covers only what makes hand-offs and headline statistics work.

Where data lives

The design rule behind everything: local systems are the source of truth; the shared node holds only what the compact agrees to share. Records enter the network one entrepreneur at a time, from real activity — never by bulk import.

Stays local · each agency

Everything, by default

  • Full case files, notes, financials
  • Custom fields and internal workflow
  • Internal record IDs and logins
  • Anything not mapped to the core

An agency's system works fully — including logins — with the network unreachable.

Shared node · compact core

The agreed minimum

  • Person — name, email, tags, consent flags
  • Organization — name, description, roles, tax status
  • Participation — typed, dated involvement (program, membership…)
  • Referral — who → whom, purpose, status, owner, follow-up
  • external_ref index — each org's own IDs, visible only to that org
The join key

external_ref

Every push carries { source, id } — the org's system name and its own record ID, never translated.

  • Same email at two orgs → records link, never duplicate
  • Each org sees only its own refs on a shared person
  • Re-sending is always safe (idempotent upserts)

Full machine-readable definitions: data-standards/v1.0/ (fields.json, enums.json — versioned, HSDS 3.0-compatible) and the OpenAPI spec in the partner packet.

Why generous beats minimal

  • A member maps once, then keeps its own words forever.
  • Unmapped fields simply aren't sent — nobody is forced to collect anything.
  • A wider shared vocabulary means richer statistics without a second data call.
  • Versioned: v1.0 → v1.1 additions are explicit, and old integrations keep working.

The group agreement — three tiers, enforced in code

Three signable, versioned documents back this — all drafted, all edited by this group before v1.0: the entrepreneur-facing Network Compact and Privacy Notice, and the staff-facing Data Usage Agreement. The tiers below are what the software actually enforces.

Always · by the compact

Operational minimum

  • Name and email (identity core)
  • Each org's own participations & referrals with the person
  • Shared tags

Visible to staff at organizations the entrepreneur actually works with — not the whole network.

Only with entrepreneur consent

Opt-in, revocable

  • Listing in the cross-org directory — off until they click the consent email (network_directory_consent)
  • One org seeing another org's records about them — asked per share (live today)
  • Named trusted-partner grants — the entrepreneur grants specific orgs a wider scope, per organization, revocable, with read / write / admin levels — shipped in the portal's "Trusted Partners (Consent Grants)" panel; see Scenario 7 (offering it upfront at intake is the open compact question)
  • Withdraw and full data-deletion request, built in
Never shared

Stays with its author

  • Internal record IDs (each org sees only its own)
  • Interaction notes — excluded even from webhooks
  • Financials, custom fields, anything unmapped

From the Network Compact (entrepreneur-facing)

"You are joining something shared by design… Each organization runs its own tools and keeps control of its own data."

"No single organization holds everyone's data… we agree on shared standards so your information can move between organizations with your permission rather than being duplicated or locked behind one system."

"Data sharing between specific organizations requires your consent each time a new organization asks for access. You can revoke access at any time."

"Organizations violating the compact can be removed from the network."

From the Privacy Notice (entrepreneur-facing)

"Each ESO only sees records they have created or that you have explicitly shared with them."

"You will always be asked before a second organization can access another organization's notes or records about you."

"We do not sell your data… Individual interaction notes are confidential between you and the recording organization."

From the Data Usage Agreement (org-facing)

"You may access entrepreneur information solely to provide ecosystem support services."

"You may not use entrepreneur data for purposes unrelated to ecosystem support, share it externally, or use it for commercial gain."

Signatures are versioned per organization; when the group revises the compact, orgs re-sign the new version.
