The API in Plain English

A regional network of entrepreneur-support organizations is being built in Connecticut. Every organization in it keeps its own system — its own CRM, its own database, its own way of working. This explains the one piece that lets those separate systems recognize the same person: what it is, what it would ask of you, and how to watch it work.

You do not need to understand the code. You need to know what it does, and roughly what it costs.

Why any of this exists

Ada tells her story five times

A founder starts a hardware company. In eighteen months she walks into a makerspace, an accelerator, a small-business lender, a state program, and a university clinic. Each one gives her an intake form. None of them knows the other four exist.

What it costs her

The same twenty questions, five times. Advice that repeats what she heard last month. Programs she qualifies for that nobody mentions, because nobody can see where she already is.

What it costs you

A referral sent by email that vanishes — you never learn whether she was seen, helped, or dropped. Reporting built by hand from five spreadsheets. And you carry an intake burden your neighbor already paid.

The fix is not another database everyone has to log into. Nobody wants a sixth system. The fix is a way for the systems you already run to hand a person to each other — and that is what the rest of this explains.

The one technical idea in this deck

A service window your software can walk up to

Every system has a front door for people: screens, forms, buttons. An API is a second door, for software. Same building, different entrance — one your CRM can use on its own, without a person clicking anything.

Three things happen in every exchange, and only three: who is asking (a key that identifies your organization, so every entry is signed), what you are telling it, and what comes back. The next slide is one real exchange, slowed down.

The payoff · read this one twice

One word did all the work: linked

The second organization did not create a second Ada. The network recognized her and answered with the same record the first organization had made — without either of them coordinating, exporting anything, or knowing the other was involved.

Nobody typed her twice

Two intakes, one shared identity. The duplicate that would have existed in every regional database simply never gets created.

Nobody logged into anything

No staff member visited a portal. Both systems did this in the background, in well under a second, as part of work that was happening anyway.

Your internal IDs stay internal

The accelerator sees rec92 — their own Airtable row. They never learn that the makerspace calls her contact 4471. Each organization reads the shared record through its own keys.

Every entry is signed

The key in that request belongs to exactly one organization. Send someone else's organization name with your key and the network refuses it. Nobody can write as anybody else.

Run the same request again tomorrow and you get updated, not a third Ada. That property — safe to repeat — is what lets an organization wire this to a Save button and stop thinking about it.

The part your board will ask about

Always shared · shared by consent · never shared

Joining does not mean opening your files. The shared record is deliberately thin, and the entrepreneur — not the organizations — controls the middle column.

Always · by the agreement

The operational minimum, visible to staff at organizations she actually works with:

  • Name and email
  • Each organization's own record of its work with her
  • Shared tags, e.g. "entrepreneur"
Only with her consent

Off by default. She opts in from a link in an email:

  • Being listed in the network directory at all
  • One organization seeing another's records about her
  • Revocable at any time; deletion is built in
Never shared

Stays inside the organization that wrote it:

  • Your internal record IDs
  • Your interaction notes
  • Financials, custom fields, anything unmapped

Notice where the consent email came from on the previous slide: the request itself asked for it. The moment a shared record is created from real activity, the entrepreneur is told and asked. There is no bulk import of anybody's contact list, ever.

Adoption · pick a rung, change it later

You can join without writing any software

The command line on slide 4 is level 2. Most organizations should start lower, and level 0 is real participation — not a waiting room.

LevelWhat your organization doesWhat you get
0 · Nothing changes Keep using email exactly as you do now. Referrals arrive with a tracking ID. Accept or decline from a link in the email — no login, no form. Less work than the follow-up you do today.
1 · Refer through the network Staff send referrals to a network address as they happen. Every referral becomes a tracked record, and the entrepreneur is asked for consent right then. The shared record set grows one real person at a time.
2 · Live sync Your system sends the request from slide 4 when staff hit Save. People and program history stay current on both sides with nobody re-typing. This is a scoped job for whoever maintains your system — there is a written playbook, a machine-readable spec, and a prompt that has an AI assistant draft the client code.
3 · Sign-in and live events Register your login server and an address for notifications. "Sign in with your organization" works on the shared tools, and events flow back into your system as they happen.

What you are not asked for: migrating off your CRM, opening your database, sharing your notes, paying per seat, or committing to anything you cannot reverse. Your system stays the system of record for your own work. If you leave, you leave with everything you brought.

The whole thing, to paste into a terminal

# 1 · get your own sandbox organization and key (invite code by email)
BASE=https://us-central1-entrepreneurship-nexus-staging.cloudfunctions.net

curl -s -X POST $BASE/provisionDemoAgency \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"name":"Your Org Name","invite_code":"ASK_FOR_THIS"}'

# 2 · add a made-up person, as your organization, from "your system"
curl -s -X POST $BASE/partnerUpsertPerson \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "X-Nexus-API-Key: PASTE_YOUR_KEY" \
  -d '{"external_ref":{"source":"my_crm","id":"contact-1"},
       "ecosystem_id":"eco_connecticut",
       "eso_org_id":"PASTE_YOUR_ORG",
       "first_name":"Ada","last_name":"Founder",
       "email":"ada.trial@example.com","tags":["entrepreneur"]}'
# → {"ok":true,"nexus_id":"…","action":"created"}
# run it again → "action":"updated", same id. no duplicate, ever.
